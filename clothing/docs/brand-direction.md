# X / MINERAL 01 — Storefront direction

6 September 2026 · Decision brief for Ayrton Mansi

## Direction implemented

**Refined active utility for people whose working day and weekend both happen in the real world.** Keep the MineralX provenance quiet. Let silhouettes, materials, practical detailing and the setting establish the identity. The initial website combines a relaxed coastal campaign with a structured product catalogue, restrained regular-weight typography and the supplied mineral palette.

This is a positioning hypothesis, not a validated market-size or demand claim. The distinctive starting point is the founder’s combination of field operations, design-led property and active outdoor life. The website translates that into connected use cases; it does not claim this aesthetic alone is a defensible moat. Any lasting advantage must come from access to real users, iterative product testing and product execution.

## Product and UX implications

The homepage opens with one coherent weekend scene and immediate collection access. Four first-collection pieces then make the product proposition tangible. Category photography broadens the use cases; the fit comparison makes the oversized-versus-fitted distinction explicit.

Men and women are independent filters inside one shared wardrobe system. Visitors can also filter by category and silhouette, search by product or colour, inspect a piece, choose a preferred size and save it to a device-local edit. Browser back/forward and deep links retain the selected catalogue view.

A pre-launch saved edit is the useful action supported by the current evidence. There is no confirmed inventory, pricing, commerce provider or launch date in the brief, so the site does not simulate payment or claim to register customer interest externally. Product availability, delivery and returns details remain release inputs.

## Visual system

- Supplied mineral colours: Chalk `#E9E6DF`, Silica `#D4D0C7`, Quartz `#B9B3A8`, Shale `#777771`, Graphite `#454744`, Carbon `#202220`, Obsidian `#111211`.
- Regular-weight, compact headlines; no oversized bold campaign typography.
- Full-bleed photography, square garment cards, thin dividers and generous space.
- Original X geometry extracted from the supplied artwork. Endorsement remains “By MineralX”.
- Local font fallbacks until licensed Suisse Int’l and Supply Mono webfonts are supplied.

## Evidence limits and research stopping point

Research used the supplied concept plus current first-party collection and product pages. It supports the design and information architecture decisions above. It does not establish conversion uplift, a market gap, final product performance, customer demand or trademark availability. No competitor imagery or product copy was copied into the storefront.

Discovery: Patagonia workwear and lululemon relaxed-fit/training pages. Follow-up: lululemon original slim-fit product and Veilance’s restrained technical positioning. The two fit propositions were independently checked on product pages before synthesis. Further competitor scanning was unlikely to change the implementation: the supplied brand system and the user’s silhouette brief determined the key choices. The next useful evidence comes from garment samples and users, rather than additional visual benchmarking.
